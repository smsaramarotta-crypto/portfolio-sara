Turning research and insights into a new e-commerce experience

UX/UI redesign of a vegan e-commerce experience, from research and accessibility to a new information architecture and responsive wireframes.

PROJECT

From site analysis to redesign decisions

The iVegan project began by analysing the existing experience to understand pain points, user needs and opportunities for improvement across the shopping journey.

Heuristic analysis, competitor research, survey, personas and user journeys guided the following phases: accessibility, information architecture review, and desktop and mobile wireframing.

ROLE

UX/UI Designer

ACTIVITIES

User Research · User Journey · Information Architecture · Accessibility · Wireframing

TOOLS USED

PROBLEM

An unclear and inconsistent experience

The analysis revealed unintuitive navigation, difficult-to-understand categories and fragmented information. The homepage and shopping journey also communicated the offering and brand values unclearly.

The research also showed how strongly transparency, reviews, prices and shipping costs influence trust and the decision to buy online.

OBJECTIVES

Making the journey clearer and more trustworthy

  • reorganise navigation, categories and information architecture;
  • make information, prices and shipping clearer;
  • strengthen brand trust and transparency;
  • simplify cart, login and checkout;
  • integrate accessibility, ethics and sustainability into the experience.

01

DISCOVERY · ANALYSIS AND RESEARCH

Identifying pain points in the existing experience

Understanding site problems and comparing them with user needs

The first phase focused on analysing the existing iVegan experience. Using Jakob Nielsen’s usability principles and the 3Cs of design, the site and app were examined to identify issues in navigation, content organisation and interaction consistency.

Competitor analysis made it possible to compare categories, product pages and purchasing features with those of other specialist e-commerce sites, highlighting opportunities for improvement.

The research was completed by defining the target audience and conducting a survey with 28 participants. The responses explored expectations and difficulties related to online shopping, confirming the importance of clear information about provenance, certifications, reviews and prices.

01

ACCESSIBILITY · PERSONAS AND USER JOURNEYS

Understanding barriers through different needs

Three perspectives for designing more inclusive journeys

The first part explored the cognitive, linguistic and visual barriers that can arise during online shopping. Three personas — Alice, Aarav and Carlo — were developed alongside as-is user journeys to observe how difficulties change according to access needs.

Alice guided the analysis of sensory overload, ambiguous language and unpredictable interactions. Aarav highlighted the importance of translation and consistent terminology. Carlo focused attention on readability, contrast, control size and compatibility with assistive technologies.

The pain points were translated into new journeys and redesign directions: more predictable navigation, direct language, earlier information about prices and shipping, and interfaces that are easier to use with different access tools.

02

ACCESSIBILITY · WCAG 2.2 ANALYSIS

Turning pain points into accessibility improvements

A focused audit of the main e-commerce areas

The second part examined five areas of the iVegan site — Homepage, product listing page (PLP), product detail page (PDP), Cart and Account — using WCAG 2.2 criteria to identify and classify the main barriers.

The analysis combined manual observation, contrast checks, keyboard-navigation tests and evaluation of shopping tasks. Recurring issues emerged around readability, focus, inconsistent behaviours, unclear names and essential information that was difficult to find.

The pain points were paired with improvement proposals for each area: stronger contrast, visible focus indicators, recognisable controls, consistent terminology, more inclusive forms, and understandable summaries of costs and discounts. The analysis mainly concerns the desktop version of the original site: during the project the site was under maintenance, so observations from earlier phases were also used.

01

WIREFRAMING · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE & DESKTOP

Building a clearer structure

From the new sitemap to the main desktop screens

Wireframing began with a review of the information architecture, reorganising categories, functions and content into a more predictable structure. Search, account, wishlist and cart become immediately recognisable, together with the main product-exploration paths.

Ethics, sustainability, certifications and Made in Italy are brought together in a dedicated hub, while mid-fidelity wireframes were developed from this new sitemap for Home, PLP, PDP, Cart and the Ethics & Sustainability Hub.

The screens directly translate the research insights: clearer hierarchy and CTAs, better-organised filters and product information, and greater visibility of totals, minimum thresholds, shipping and discounts on the way to checkout.

02

WIREFRAMING · MOBILE & WIREFLOW

Adapting the journey to mobile

From desktop screens to touch-focused interactions

The mobile version was redesigned by prioritising key actions instead of simply reducing the desktop layout. Navigation, categories and controls were adapted to the available space and touch interactions.

Filters and sorting become more compact, while PDP information is organised progressively through a simpler hierarchy. In the cart, the elements that influence the purchase decision — such as total, shipping, thresholds and discounts — remain immediately visible.

Finally, the wireflow checked continuity from category to PLP and PDP, through cart, authentication and checkout, ensuring that each screen worked as a coherent part of the same flow.

CONCLUSION

From research to a clearer and more accessible experience

The project transformed pain points around navigation, transparency and accessibility into a new experience structure, connecting user research, user journeys, WCAG requirements and information architecture.

The accessibility work expanded this perspective, showing how the same pain points can become real barriers for people with cognitive, language or visual access needs. Comparing the experience with WCAG 2.2 therefore turned the insights into more concrete requirements for readability, interaction, comprehension and compatibility with assistive technologies.

The result is a clearer, more consistent and inclusive functional foundation, developed through desktop and mobile wireframes and ready to be translated into the next Visual Design phase.
