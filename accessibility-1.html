ACCESSIBILITY 01 · iVEGAN

Understanding barriers through people and journeys

Three project personas explore the iVegan shopping experience from cognitive, language and visual perspectives.

The as-is journey analysis connects recurring barriers to clearer, more predictable and accessible redesign scenarios.

01 · PERSONAS

Three perspectives, three access needs

The personas represent usage scenarios defined for the project: Alice focuses on cognitive and sensory barriers, Aarav on language barriers, and Carlo on low vision and assistive technologies. They are not accounts of individual interviews.

UNIVERSITY STUDENT · AUTISM

Alice

She wants to buy vegetarian and vegan products while checking that they align with her values.

Need

Linear navigation, explicit information and predictable interactions.

Journey barrier

Decorative elements and ambiguous categories make it difficult to find her way; the cart is lost after sign-up.

Opportunity

Reduce sensory load, use direct language and preserve the cart during sign-up.

INTERNATIONAL STUDENT · NON-NATIVE SPEAKER

Aarav

He is looking for vegan products in Italy and wants to be able to understand the site in English as well.

Need

Localised content and terminology that immediately describes products and actions.

Journey barrier

Complete translations are missing; categories such as “Mare e Monti” and “Vegmaggio”, along with checkout fields, may be difficult to understand.

Opportunity

Provide complete language versions, explicit categories and understandable forms.

LOW-VISION PERSON · ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGIES

Carlo

He wants to complete the purchase using a keyboard, screen reader and magnification.

Need

Sufficient contrast, keyboard control and feedback that can be read by assistive technologies.

Journey barrier

Difficult-to-read text and controls, elements that cannot be reached with TAB, and incomplete error messages.

Opportunity

Semantic structure, visible focus, accessible labels and a persistent cart.

02 · AS-IS USER JOURNEY

Where the journey becomes difficult

The three scenarios reconstruct comparable activities — search, navigation, cart, account and purchase — and highlight different barriers within the same flow.

Alice · cognitive

AS-IS
  1. Discovery

    Having a separate website and shop requires additional orientation.

  2. Exploration

    Unclear categories and dense content increase overload.

  3. Cart

    Actions and costs are not always communicated clearly.

  4. Sign-up

    The cart may be emptied after creating an account.

  5. Confirmation

    The “order placed” message may not immediately clarify the delivery method.

Aarav · language

AS-IS
  1. Discovery

    He cannot find a complete option for browsing the site in English.

  2. Categories

    Names such as “Mare e Monti” do not make it clear which products they contain.

  3. Products

    He has to translate ingredients and descriptions himself.

  4. Cart

    The minimum threshold and shipping conditions are not immediately clear.

  5. Checkout

    Forms and instructions may slow down completion.

Carlo · visual

AS-IS
  1. Access

    Navigation depends on magnification and keyboard access.

  2. Categories

    Some content is not easy to use with a screen reader.

  3. Products

    Controls and confirmations when adding to the cart are not always announced.

  4. Account

    Error messages and fields may not be interpreted by assistive tools.

  5. Purchase

    Losing the cart and the absence of feedback make the flow discontinuous.

CROSS-CUTTING INSIGHT

The difficulties are not limited to individual components: a lack of clarity, fragmented channels and incomplete feedback can compromise autonomy throughout the shopping journey.

03 · PAIN POINTS & OPPORTUNITIES

From barrier to design requirement

The presentation connects cognitive, language and visual barriers to interface proposals. The pairings summarise redesign directions, not findings from later testing.

PAIN POINTS Sensory overload

Animations, dense information and categories that are difficult to interpret.

→
PROPOSAL Predictable experience

Consistent layout, essential content, direct instructions and optional further details.

PAIN POINTS Language and terminology

No English version and abstract commercial terms.

→
PROPOSAL Understandable content

Complete translation, explicit labels and localised forms.

PAIN POINTS Visual access

Low contrast, controls that are difficult to recognise and unannounced interactions.

→
PROPOSAL Access with assistive technologies

Semantic HTML, visible focus, clear labels and feedback that can be announced.

PAIN POINTS Checkout flow

Unclear shipping and a cart that is lost after sign-up.

→
PROPOSAL Journey continuity

Visible totals and thresholds, autocomplete, persistent cart and explicit confirmations.

04 · NEW USER JOURNEY

How the experience could change

The new journeys illustrate proposed design scenarios for Alice, Aarav and Carlo. They do not document implemented changes or results measured through usability testing.

Alice

PROPOSAL
  1. Search

    Access to a unified site with clear brand information.

  2. Navigation

    Essential layout and descriptive categories.

  3. Products

    Transparent information and a recognisable add-to-cart action.

  4. Account

    Simplified login, understandable fields and a preserved cart.

  5. Confirmation

    Cost and delivery type communicated explicitly.

Aarav

PROPOSAL
  1. Language

    English version available from the homepage.

  2. Catalogue

    Categories and product pages translated consistently.

  3. Cart

    Totals, order minimums and shipping that are easy to understand.

  4. Checkout

    Localised fields and address autocomplete.

  5. Confirmation

    Order summary available in the selected language.

Carlo

PROPOSAL
  1. Discovery

    Semantic structure usable with a screen reader.

  2. Exploration

    Content that can be magnified and controls reachable by keyboard.

  3. Cart

    Accessible feedback and amounts that can be announced.

  4. Account

    Form labels and validation compatible with assistive tools.

  5. Order

    Unchanged cart and accessible confirmations.
