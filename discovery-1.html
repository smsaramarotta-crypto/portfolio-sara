DISCOVERY 01 · AS-IS ANALYSIS AND COMPETITORS

Understanding pain points in the existing experience

Before starting the redesign, the iVegan site was analysed to understand where the experience was unclear, inconsistent or difficult to navigate.

The research combined heuristic analysis, the 3Cs of design, a visual sitemap, competitor benchmarking and a survey with target users.

01 · HEURISTIC ANALYSIS

Evaluating the existing experience

The site was analysed through Jakob Nielsen’s 10 usability principles across desktop, tablet, mobile and app.

The analysis identified elements that were already effective, such as system feedback and filter options, as well as recurring pain points: unintuitive categories, important information that was difficult to see, inconsistent interactions and journeys that required too much effort to understand.

Visibility of system status

  • Product availability is not always clear.
  • Free-shipping information is difficult to find.
  • Cart status is not always visible in the app.

Match between the system and the real world

  • Categories are not self-explanatory enough.
  • Content hierarchy is not very logical.
  • Shipping information is fragmented.

User control and freedom

  • Scroll position was not preserved when going back.
  • The chat opened even after “No thanks” and was difficult to close.
  • Sliders and navigation offered controls that were difficult to identify.

Consistency and standards

  • Categories had no alphabetical or logical order.
  • Hover states and buttons behaved differently visually.
  • Product pages and icons were inconsistent between the site and app.

Error prevention

  • “Accept” and “Decline” looked too similar.
  • Click targets were too small in categories and the mobile cart.
  • Some form choices could cause errors or exclusion.

Recognition rather than recall

  • Important information was scattered across different places.
  • Categories were difficult to find again because they lacked a clear logic.
  • Required fields and functions were not always immediately recognisable.

Flexibility and efficiency of use

  • Some shortcuts led to inefficient paths.
  • The “Back to top” button could be covered by the chat.
  • The homepage and navigation increased cognitive load.

Aesthetic and minimalist design

  • Visual identity was inconsistent.
  • Spacing, boxes and images had inconsistent sizes and styles.
  • Several images appeared blurred or cropped in the app.

Recognising, diagnosing and recovering from errors

  • Some errors did not clearly explain how to continue.
  • The minimum-order message did not offer a quick way back to shopping.
  • Some pages contained incorrect or test content.

Help and documentation

  • “Secure payment” did not explain why the payment was secure.
  • The loyalty programme was explained in a fragmented way.
  • Some account functions lacked clear instructions.

02 · 3Cs OF DESIGN

Clarity, content and consistency

Reading the experience through the 3Cs summarised the pain points in three areas: communication clarity, content organisation and interface consistency.

The as-is visual sitemap then made the fragmented structure visible, revealing redundancies, non-linear paths and content that was difficult to reach.

03 · COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Comparing iVegan with specialist e-commerce sites

The benchmark compared iVegan with direct competitors specialising in the vegan market, examining site structure, account and cart, product boxes and key trust factors.

The comparison showed generally more consistent and complete experiences, particularly in cart management, product-page consistency and communication of shopping information.

04 · TARGET & SURVEY

Validating priorities and expectations with real users

The target audience was explored through demographic and sociographic data and then validated with a survey of vegan and vegetarian people aged 18 to 35.

The questionnaire explored online shopping, product availability, transparency, trust and the main frustrations in the shopping process.

KEY INSIGHT

Trust depends on clarity.

Product origin and processing, certifications, reviews, prices and shipping must be immediately understandable. At the same time, navigation and access should require as little effort as possible.
