ACCESSIBILITY 02 · iVEGAN

From barriers to accessibility requirements

The second part examines the Homepage, product listing, product detail, cart and account areas using WCAG 2.2 as a reference.

The review combines manual observation, contrast checks, keyboard navigation and analysis of key tasks, with improvement actions organised by area.

01 · METHOD AND SCOPE

A manual audit based on WCAG 2.2

The project examines five representative areas of the iVegan site and documents issues identified in their use. The presentation classifies the referenced WCAG requirements by level: A, AA and AAA.

01

Interface observation

Review of navigation, naming, content hierarchy and component behaviour.

02

Contrast and perception

Review of contrast ratios and the readability of text, labels and controls.

03

Keyboard and tasks

Exploration with TAB and evaluation of the cart, checkout and personal-data management.

LIMITATIONS OF THE REVIEW

At the time of analysis, the site was under maintenance. The presentation explains that the audit is mainly based on observations from the Discovery phases, complemented by the Wayback Machine archive and selected references to the app. It therefore does not constitute a complete certification of the site’s current state.

02 · AREAS ANALYSED

Five key areas of the e-commerce experience

The review covers the experience from first access through account management, highlighting a recurring issue and related redesign direction for each section.

Homepage Product Listing Page Product Details Page Cart Account
01

HOME

Homepage

Top bar, menu, hero and informational sections.

PAIN POINTS

Inconsistent contrast and readability; sliders and messages that may interrupt reading; navigation structure that is not always predictable.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Increase contrast, slow down or control sliders, and make menus and CTAs recognisable and keyboard accessible.

02

PLP

Product Listing Page

Categories, filters and product listing.

PAIN POINTS

Categories and subcategories are not always ordered or immediately clear; product pages are inconsistent; information is distributed unevenly.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Reorganise categories, make filters more visible, and standardise information and controls across cards.

03

PDP

Product Details Page

Product detail, availability and shopping actions.

PAIN POINTS

Inconsistent naming and feedback; unclear availability and product information; some interactive areas are too small.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Explicit availability states, readable text, recognisable CTAs, and controls with suitable targets and focus.

04

CART

Cart

Summary, discounts, thresholds and checkout.

PAIN POINTS

Costs, minimum spend, free-shipping threshold and vouchers are not always immediately visible; action paths are inconsistent.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Clear summary, consistent CTAs, announced checkout states and understandable voucher application.

05

ACCOUNT

Account

Profile, sign-in and discount vouchers.

PAIN POINTS

Forms, required fields and error messages are not always clear; voucher management is unintuitive.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Explicit labels, accessible validation, understandable password recovery and recognisable voucher management.

03 · WCAG PRINCIPLES

Four principles for reading the pain points

WCAG organises accessibility around four principles. The presentation uses these references to connect interface observations to verifiable requirements.

01 · PERCEIVABLE

Perceivable

Contrast, size and information must make content and actions recognisable.

Esempio: 1.4.3 · Contrast (Minimum) · AA
02 · OPERABLE

Operable

Menus and controls must work with a keyboard and other input methods.

Visible focus, logical navigation order and recognisable targets.
03 · UNDERSTANDABLE

Understandable

Category names, instructions, errors and status messages must be clear.

Consistent labels and information across pages.
04 · ROBUST

Robust

Markup and interactions must be interpretable by browsers and assistive technologies.

Semantic structure and communication of states.

The presentation includes the principles and criterion 1.4.3. This analysis does not establish a single score or certify the site’s legal compliance.

04 · FROM AUDIT TO REDESIGN

Turning barriers into targeted actions

The directions collected across the different pages converge around several cross-cutting priorities. The visuals summarise proposals from the project, not changes that have already been implemented.

PAIN POINTS Low contrast and difficult-to-read messages

Essential information is not sufficiently perceivable.

→
PROPOSAL Contrast and hierarchy

More readable text and buttons, clear messages and identifiable states.

PAIN POINTS Inconsistent focus and interactions

Menus, cart and controls are difficult to use with TAB.

→
PROPOSAL Keyboard navigation

Logical order, visible focus and reachable components.

PAIN POINTS Unclear naming

Categories, labels and availability are not explicit enough.

→
PROPOSAL Understandable language

Direct terms, consistent breadcrumbs and predictable feedback.

PAIN POINTS Ambiguous forms and costs

Required fields, discounts and thresholds are unclear.

→
PROPOSAL Accessible checkout

Recognisable validation, visible summaries and accessible notifications.

RESEARCH OUTPUT

Directions applicable to the redesign

01 Readability

Clearer contrast and hierarchy for essential content.

02 Interaction

Consistent keyboard access, focus and feedback across key tasks.

03 Comprehension

Explicit labels, forms and summaries that are easy to interpret.
